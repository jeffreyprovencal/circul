3. Processor Supply Requirements

Processors define what they need from their aggregators — driven by client purchase orders

Who creates: Processors only (based on POs from their recycler/converter/export clients)
Who sees: Their aggregators and (through cascade) their collectors
Key idea: The processor is the quality gatekeeper. They translate client POs into supply requirements that flow downward.
Miniplast case: As a vertically integrated processor+recycler+converter, they just set requirements at their processor account — the chain below sees the same specs regardless.

4. Requirement Visibility — What Your Processor Needs

Aggregators and collectors see processor requirements so they know what to collect and how

Aggregator view: Sees full processor requirements with accepted forms (Loose, Bales, Flakes) and quality thresholds. Knows exactly what to deliver.
Collector view: Sees simplified version — material, colour, and the "Loose" form they need to meet. Knows what to sort for and the premium available.
Key insight: A collector in Accra now sees that rePATRN Ghana needs Clear PET at <3% contamination with a 5% premium. They don't need to know about PO numbers or downstream clients — just what to collect and how to earn more.

5. Spec-Linked Transactions

Tag incoming deliveries against your supply requirements for quality traceability

Where: In the processor's delivery queue (pending transactions)
How: When reviewing an incoming delivery from an aggregator, the processor tags it against one of their supply requirements: "Meets", "Partial", or "Below"
Why: Creates a quality traceability chain — each batch has a compliance record linked to a specific supply requirement. This is what partners pay premium for.
